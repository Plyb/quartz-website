If you're reading this, I probably sent you an email. **I wrote that email myself**, not using AI[^1], but it might seem like it has some AI-like features. Here's why:
- I intend my emails to be highly **"skimmable"**
	- **Your time is valuable**. I want you to spend as much or as little time on my email as you feel appropriate
	- I put the **most important elements** in **high level bullet points** with details in sub-bullet points. This allow you to only read those sub-bullets if you feel the need to.
	- I also put **key phrases** in **bold**
		- This provides anchors while you are reading
		- It also allows you to quickly visually review to see if you missed anything important
# Why I write this way
- It is intentional, and influenced by experiences I've had
	- I like Neel Nanda's writing style ([example](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/49-mentoring)), and I strive to emulate it
	- I've received feedback that this style is helpful for application reviewers
- It's also possible I have unconsciously picked up some AI-style habits
	- I spend a lot of time interacting with LLMs
	- I try to maintain my own unique voice! Feel free to point out to me if you see me slipping into AI voice
# Why I wrote this post
- I don't want to read slop, and I don't think you do either. But **I want you to ready my email**!
	- While AI *style* can be "well-written" in some sense, I find pure AI-generated text to be off-putting. I believe this is because the *content* of AI-generated text is often not as valuable.
		- It's often fluffy
		- There is a nagging concern about hallucinations

[^1]: I may occasionally use it to review what I write, but if I linked to this post, that means I *at least* wrote the first draft myself. At the time of writing this, I also hardly use AI for review, but may sometimes.
